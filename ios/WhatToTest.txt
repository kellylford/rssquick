Welcome to RSS Quick for iPhone and iPad. This is the initial release, covering the basics of reading RSS: a simple feed reader built for VoiceOver, braille displays and anyone who wants to get through a lot of headlines quickly.

What it does
- Opens with a starter list of news, business, technology, science and health, culture, sports and accessibility feeds, arranged in folders.
- Tap a folder to open or close it. Tap a feed to see its headlines, newest first.
- To load every feed in a folder at once, use the Show all headlines action on the folder (swipe up or down with VoiceOver), or long-press it and choose Show All Headlines. The merged list names the feed each headline came from.
- Tap a headline to read the article in Safari inside the app. Done brings you back to the same headline.
- Use your own feeds: in the Feed List menu at the top of the screen, choose Import OPML File to open one from Files, then Make This My Default Feed List so it opens every time. Use Starter Feed List goes back.
- Refresh, in a list of headlines, fetches them again.
- Nothing is tracked and nothing is stored except the feed list you choose to keep. Headlines are fetched fresh each time.

What to try
- Move around with VoiceOver: folders, feeds, headlines and the Feed List menu should all say clearly what they are and whether a folder is open.
- Load a folder of feeds. VoiceOver should announce how many headlines arrived, and how many feeds failed if any did.
- Open an article and come back. You should land on the headline you left.
- Import an OPML file exported from another feed reader, and make it your default.
- Try it with a braille display, and with larger text sizes.

Tell us what you find with the Send Beta Feedback option in TestFlight, or at https://github.com/kellylford/rssquick/issues. Anything about VoiceOver, braille or reading is especially welcome. You do not need to know the cause; what you heard, or could not reach, is the useful part.
